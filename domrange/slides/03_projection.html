Projection: Domain and Range Types

$\cTDom{F}$ and $\cTRange{F}$ are first-class types.
On a known arrow type, they eliminate:
$\cTDom{A \to B} \equiv A \qquad\qquad \cTRange{A \to B} \equiv B$
$\cTDom{\TInt}$ is well-defined too, but does not eliminate.
With arrow variance, $F <: A \to B$ gives:
$\chl{A \equiv \cTDom{A \to B} <: \cTDom{F}} \qquad \cTRange{F} <: \cTRange{A \to B} \equiv B$
An upper bound on $F$ gives its domain a lower bound.

More Than Type Parameters

$\cTDom{F}$ and $\cTRange{F}$ cannot simply be replaced by type parameters $A$ and $B$.
$T <: \cTDom{F}$
a lower bound, by contravariance
$\forall X <: T.\ \ldots$
F<: has upper bounds only
So we can construct programs that F<: cannot type:
$g \;:=\; \Lambda F <: \TBool \to \TBool.\ \lambda f : F.\ \lambda h : \chl{\cTRange{F} \to \cTDom{F}}.\ f(h(f(\textsf{true})))$
F<: splits $F$ into $A$ and $B$ to type $h$, but then $\textsf{true}$ needs the underivable $\TBool <: A$:
$g' \;:=\; \Lambda A <: \TTop.\ \Lambda B <: \TBool.\ \lambda f : A \to B.\ \lambda h : \chl{B \to A}.\ f(h(f({\cer \textsf{true}})))$
